## 2. The problem

Preparing for IELTS is expensive if you do it right and ineffective if you don't. A human tutor who can grade your writing against the four official criteria, conduct a realistic speaking exam, and tell you exactly what you're doing wrong costs money most candidates don't have and time that's hard to schedule. The available alternatives fall short in ways that matter:

- **Generic practice apps don't replicate the real exam.** Mock tests without a realistic exam environment — timed, invigilated, format-faithful — produce candidates who know the content but not the pressure. The exam has specific time constraints, question formats, and section structures. Familiarity with those isn't optional.
- **AI writing feedback is usually a chat window.** Most AI writing tools generate text in a side panel. The candidate pastes their essay in, gets a comment back, and pastes any revision in manually. For structured IELTS preparation, what matters is per-criterion band scoring, a model answer to compare against, and targeted identification of recurring errors — not a generic rewrite suggestion.
- **Speaking practice without an AI examiner doesn't transfer.** Reading about speaking techniques doesn't build the reflex. Candidates need to speak, be listened to, and receive criterion-specific feedback across all four official IELTS Speaking marking descriptors — fluency and coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy, pronunciation.
- **Progress tracking is manual or absent.** Without automatic error extraction and trend analysis, candidates don't know whether they're actually improving on their weak points or just practicing their strong ones.

## 4. Writing — the most comprehensive module

Writing is the skill candidates struggle with most and get the least useful feedback on. The Writing module is the deepest part of the platform.

### Mock tests and free practice

- **Writing Mock Test** — Full timed mock with Task 1 and Task 2 together, Cambridge-style prompts, real chart images for Academic Task 1, and auto-save. Single-task mocks (Task 1 only or Task 2 only) are also available.
- **Writing Lab** — Free-form practice: the candidate enters their own prompt and writes against a 60-minute timer. The same AI grading pipeline applies.

### AI feedback and scoring

After any submission — mock, lab session, or mentor chat — the candidate receives:

- Band score per criterion (Task Achievement/Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy)
- Overall band score
- Examiner-style written comment
- A Band 9 model answer rewrite for comparison

### AI mentors — coaching by section

Two conversational AI coaches, one per task type:

- **Task 1 Mentor** — Academic Task 1. Choose a chart type and mode (Introduction Only or Full Response). Real-time streaming with a resizable split pane on web for side-by-side writing and coaching.
- **Task 2 Mentor** — Task 2 essays. Choose essay type (opinion, discussion, problem/solution, two-part, advantage/disadvantage) and mode (Introduction Only or Full Essay by section). Detailed structural feedback with band-level identification for each section.

### Introduction Mastery course

A mini-course covering all five Task 2 essay types, an essay-type identification quiz, and free-practice rounds with AI feedback. Targets the specific sub-skill that blocks most candidates from Band 7+: writing an introduction that correctly identifies the question type and signals the right essay structure.

### Mistake Practice and history

- **Writing Practice History** — Unified timeline of all writing activity (essays, AI mentor chats, and mocks) with filters for Task 1, Task 2, and Mock.
- **Mistake Practice** — Drill specifically on weak areas identified by the Mistake Vault. AI verdict on each answer with sound cues on success.

## 5. Speaking — a fully AI-conducted exam

Speaking is the hardest IELTS skill to practice alone. The Speaking module makes it possible.

- **Speaking Mock Test** — A full 3-part AI-conducted exam with randomly selected examiner personas. Part 1 (introduction and interview), Part 2 (long turn with cue card), Part 3 (discussion). Silence detection auto-submits after 6 seconds — matching real exam conditions. An animated orb shows microphone activity throughout. Scored across all four official criteria.
- **Fun Practice** — Casual conversation with a friendly AI tutor. No structured format. Speak freely and receive inline vocabulary and grammar tips. Voice or text input.
- **Speaking History** — Full log of all speaking sessions with overall band scores and dates. Tap any session to revisit the complete feedback report.

---

## 6. Reading — full exam interface

- **Practice and Mock Test Library** — Academic and General Training tests showing the candidate's best previous band score, question count, and duration per test. Premium tests clearly labelled.
- **Reading Lab** — Full exam interface with a resizable split pane (passage and questions side by side), all official question types (MCQ, True/False/Not Given, Yes/No/Not Given, Short Answer, Fill in Blanks, Matching Headings, Matching Information), a question palette, floating timer, and an answer review panel before submission.

The resizable split pane is specifically a desktop web feature — it matches how candidates interact with the passage and questions in the real computer-based exam.

---

## 7. Listening — exact exam conditions

- **Mock Mode** — 30 minutes, 4 parts. Audio plays automatically and advances per section. No rewinding — exactly like the real exam. Animated waveform visualiser throughout.
- **Practice Mode** — Full audio controls: play/pause, restart, ±15s seek, scrubable progress bar. 10-minute timer per part. Auto-saves answers and position so the candidate can resume where they left off.

The distinction between the two modes is deliberate and important. Exam simulation requires restrictions (no rewind) that would be counterproductive in practice. Both serve their purpose because they're kept separate.

## 8. Signature feature: the Mistake Vault

This is the feature that separates IELTS Tutors from generic practice apps. Most platforms tell you what your band score is. The Mistake Vault tells you why it isn't higher — and whether you're getting worse or better at each specific error type.

**How it works:**
- Errors from every Writing and Speaking session are automatically extracted and categorised by AI. No manual logging required.
- Each tracked error shows: how often it has appeared, across how many distinct sessions, and a trend badge — **Worsening**, **Improving**, or **Stable**.
- From any error entry, the candidate can hit Practise to drill it immediately.

The trend badges are the critical piece. A candidate who sees "article misuse — Worsening — 14 occurrences across 7 sessions" knows exactly where to focus next. A candidate who sees "subject-verb agreement — Improving — 3 occurrences across 2 sessions (down from 9)" knows their correction is working.

## 10. Gamification and study planning

Consistent daily practice is the single biggest predictor of IELTS improvement. The gamification layer is designed around that fact:

- **Daily streak** — Study every day to build a streak, shown on home and profile. Sound cue plays the first time the streak increments each day.
- **Target band** — Set a target (4.0–9.0 in 0.5 steps). The app tracks progress toward it and personalises motivational copy.
- **Level-up milestones** — Celebratory sound when the candidate first hits their target band or crosses a major milestone (7.0, 8.0, 9.0). Fires once per skill — never stale.
- **Study Scheduler** — Weekly schedule with task creation (skill, time, duration, repeat pattern), conflict detection, edit/delete by occurrence or series, and 5-minute push notification reminders.

The scheduler's conflict detection is a small but important detail. A candidate who sets overlapping sessions gets a warning before saving, not a silent collision that corrupts their plan.

## 14. Before you publish

**Claim discipline:**
- "Instant" AI band scoring is accurate for writing and speaking grading pipelines — confirm average latency and qualify if needed (e.g. "typically under X seconds").
- "Full exam parity" claims for Speaking (silence detection, examiner personas, 3-part structure) are accurate per the source. Confirm the audio pipeline handles varied microphone hardware reliably before leading with it.
- iOS is listed as "in progress." Do not describe it as available until it is on the App Store.
- Do not claim user numbers, average band improvement, or pass rates without measured data.
- The testimonials section, if present, should use real candidate quotes only — not illustrative personas.
